Laying the Foundation, Day 4 Engine Heartbeat

October 22, 2025, Written by BlackFrame AI Studio

Production timeline

Primary log captured June 11, 2025 — Day 4 when the engine loop locked into a steady heartbeat.

Builds extend from the scaffold bootstrapped on June 7, 2025, after Day 1’s governance pass on June 8 and the runtime groundwork laid on Day 2 and Day 3.

Day 4 marked the moment the Crescent Veil engine stopped being a static scaffold and started breathing on its own. We spent the entire dispatch inside the boiler room: codifying the directory layout, letting Codex spin up the repetitive stubs, and then threading those pieces into a deterministic startup, update, and shutdown rhythm. No pixels rendered yet—those arrive on Day 7—but our consoles now thrum with the first steady heartbeat.

Build verification

Engine loop online without manual patches

Every subsystem invoked today—EngineController, RendererFacade, EventQueue, ResourceLoader, InputRouter, and SceneDirector—was generated or expanded by Codex, audited against the Scaffold Style Checklist, and wired into the main loop without hand-written rescues. Human review focused on naming, dependency hygiene, and runtime assertions.

  • Runtime boot log captures initialization order, subsystem health, and loop cadence.
  • Codex-authored stubs are tracked in the Devlog Vault alongside policy notes documenting our adjustments.

The result is a verifiable core that ticks reliably while we stage the rendering and gameplay payloads.

Morning: Cleaning the Blueprint Skeleton

The first block of work honored the promise we made on Day 2: never let Codex’s rapid scaffolding drift from our canon. We audited namespaces, directory names, and include guards across the fresh engine tree, aligning everything to the cv:: module scheme. Codex handled the rote renames, while we trimmed duplicate math helpers and reconciled the configuration headers so every subsystem shares the same compile-time switches.

“Codex touched 17 headers during the rename pass; each change references the Module Registry and ships with matching documentation updates.”

By mid-morning the repository tree finally mirrored the architectural diagram hanging over the war table. That clarity keeps the next waves of generation honest and reduces the review burden when we hand transcripts to partners.

Midday: Bootstrapping the Core Loop

With the filesystem tidy, we summoned Codex to flesh out the EngineController. The agent scaffolded lifecycle hooks (initialize(), run(), shutdown()) plus a deterministic TickTimer wrapper that we bound to our chrono policy. We inserted assertions that verify every subsystem registers before the loop begins, then let Codex author the top-level main() that hands off to the controller and captures exit codes for tooling.

The inaugural run spelled out the cadence we needed: init banners, subsystem readiness checks, then a clean “tick…tick…tick…” streaming at 60Hz. The executable now exits gracefully on signal, flushing telemetry and releasing the placeholder renderer.

Afternoon: Systems Wired for Future Payloads

Once the heartbeat stabilized, we asked Codex to stub the systems that will matter when rendering comes online. RendererFacade exposes command buffers and swap hooks without binding to a concrete backend yet. ResourceLoader enumerates asset roots and validates manifest signatures, returning default handles for now. InputRouter listens to dummy events and queues them through EventQueue so the game layer can subscribe later. We documented a SceneDirector sketch in Markdown, fed it through Codex, and committed the resulting interface alongside placeholder scenes.

Every stub runs inside the main loop already, which means filling in real implementations later won’t require surgical refactors. The systems announce their status on boot, participate in the update step, and tear down cleanly—guarding us against the last-minute bolting we swore to avoid.

Evening: Instrumentation Becomes Our Display

Without pixels to look at, we treated logging as our dashboard. Codex generated a LogChannel utility with severity filters and structured formatting. We layered in human-written policies for thread safety and rate limiting, then piped every subsystem through the logger. The console now reports per-tick timing, queue depths, and watchdog heartbeats. It’s humble, but it means we already know what “healthy” looks like before the renderer ever spins a frame.

We closed the day by snapshotting the transcripts, noting Day 5 objectives (attach the GLFW backend, integrate the math library bindings), and flagging questions for Codex about asset hot-loading. The AI is prepped with context; tomorrow we’ll turn those TODOs into working code.
